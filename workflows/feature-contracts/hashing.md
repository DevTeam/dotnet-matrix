# Hashing feature contract

## Scope

The category compares non-cryptographic hash and checksum implementations over
in-memory buffers. Cryptography, key derivation, password hashing, HMAC, file
and network I/O, and hardware-specific intrinsics selection are outside scope.

**One algorithm per scenario.** A scenario compares implementations of the same
hash function, so a result difference is an implementation difference and never
an algorithm difference. A library that implements a different algorithm is
`Unsupported` for that scenario — never "slower". This rule is what makes the
category meaningful at all: BLAKE3 and CRC32 have no common notion of speed.

Corpus generation, seed setup, expected-value calculation, and hasher
construction (except where a scenario measures it) happen before measurement.

## Corpus

Two deterministic corpora from `HashData`, both generated by a 64-bit LCG
(`state * 6364136223846793005 + 1442695040888963407`, byte taken from the high
bits), so every machine and every run sees identical bytes without storing them
in the repository:

| Name | Length | Seed | First 8 bytes |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Small | 4 096 bytes | `0x5EED0017` | `B345E80FC54F75CD` |
| Large | 1 MiB | `0x5EED0029` | `E99BAEBA2F390192` |

All hashes in this contract use seed `0` unless a scenario says otherwise. A
zero seed is the value every competing API accepts, which keeps the call shape
identical across libraries.

## Scenarios

Each scenario hashes the small corpus unless stated otherwise and returns the
raw integer result in a strongly typed value.

| # | Feature | Operation | Algorithm | Rated |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `XxHash64` | one-shot over 4 096 bytes | xxHash64 | yes |
| 2 | `XxHash32` | one-shot over 4 096 bytes | xxHash32 | yes |
| 3 | `XxHash3` | one-shot 64-bit over 4 096 bytes | xxHash3 | yes |
| 4 | `XxHash64 Streaming` | 64 incremental updates of 64 bytes | xxHash64 | yes |
| 5 | `XxHash64 Large` | one-shot over 1 MiB | xxHash64 | yes |
| 6 | `Crc32` | one-shot over 4 096 bytes | CRC-32 (IEEE) | yes |
| 7 | `Blake3` | one-shot over 4 096 bytes | BLAKE3 | no |
| 8 | `Blake2b` | one-shot 32-byte digest over 4 096 bytes | BLAKE2b | no |
| 9 | `FarmHash64` | one-shot over 4 096 bytes | FarmHash64 | no |

### Why six scenarios are rated and three are not

Scenarios 1, 2, 5 and 6 are contested by four rated libraries; scenario 3 by
two; scenario 4 by two. Scenarios 7, 8 and 9 have **exactly one implementing
library**, and `workflows/rating.md` records that as the one mathematical case
that earns `rated: false`: with a single entrant `best` is that library's own
result, `ratio = 1`, and it scores the full 200 points whatever the number is.
A race with one runner is not a race.

The same document fixes the other side of the line: "a low percentage is
necessary to notice a candidate, never sufficient to flag one". Scenarios 3 and
4 are contested by two libraries each, which is the precedent recorded for
`JsonSerialization / Polymorphic Round Trip` (2 of 3), `Validation / Stop On
First Failure` (2 of 4) and `LinqQueries / Span Source` (2 of 5). All three stay
rated, and so do scenarios 3 and 4.

The three single-algorithm libraries are visible, validated and measured; they
simply do not enter the standing:

| Library | Why it is not rated | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Blake3` | The only BLAKE3 implementation; scenario 7 has one entrant. | `With this few rated entrants, the reference is a library's own result, not a result earned against a competitor, so the full 200 points would not reflect a win.` |
| `Isopoh.Cryptography.Blake2b` | The only BLAKE2b implementation; scenario 8 has one entrant. | same reason |
| `FarmHash.Sharp` | The only FarmHash implementation; scenario 9 has one entrant. | same reason |

They also carry `MatrixRating=false`, which `rating.md` calls "a reference, with
a dash instead of a score". `workflows/add-library.md` defines that flag for
"when an ordinary library must stay visible but excluded", and requires no
baseline: `MatrixBaseline` stays absent. A library in no rated scenario must not
be shown as scoring nothing of the category maximum, and `MatrixRating.Create`
starts from `report.Libraries.Where(library => Competes(library.Id))`, so these
three are absent from the category standing rather than present with a zero.
Their visibility comes from the feature matrix, which reports `FeatureStatus`
per library independently of the benchmarks.

**Maximum: `200 x 6 = 1200`** — six rated scenarios, 200 points each.

### Chart groups

`charts.json` holds groups of feature IDs. A group is a curated view of
scenarios, not a partition of libraries and not a partition of algorithms; the
algorithm is expressed by the scenario, which is why scenarios 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5
are five separate features instead of one "xxHash" scenario.

| Group | Name | Scenarios | Libraries with complete coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hash` | Hash | 1, 2 | 4 |
| `large-input` | Large Input | 5 | 4 |
| `streaming` | Streaming | 4 | 2 |
| `checksum` | Checksum | 6 | 2 |
| `wide-hash` | Wide Hash | 3 | 2 |

A group ranks a library only when it covers the group's complete feature set, so
each group is built to keep at least two competitors in it. `wide-hash` holds
scenario 3 alone: adding it to `hash` would drop `K4os.Hash.xxHash` and
`HashDepot` out of that group's standing for a reason unrelated to hashing a
small buffer, and combining it with `checksum` would leave a single row.

Scenarios 7, 8 and 9 are in no group, which is what `rated: false` means here:
like `JsonSerialization / Source Generation Round Trip`,
`Validation / Async Validation` and `LinqQueries / ValueDelegateFilter`, they
are drawn by their own per-feature chart and by no overview group.

### Reasons for the gaps

- **`K4os.Hash.xxHash` and `HashDepot` without `XxHash3`.** Neither package
  exposes an xxHash3 function. The public surface of the installed version was
  read rather than the package description assumed: `K4os.Hash.xxHash` 1.0.8
  declares `XXH32` and `XXH64` only, and `HashDepot` 3.2.0 declares
  `XXHash.Hash32`, `XXHash.Hash64`, `MurmurHash3`, `Fnv1a` and `SipHash24` —
  `HashDepot.XXHash.Hash64` is xxHash64.
- **`Standart.Hash.xxHash` and `HashDepot` without `XxHash64 Streaming`.** Both
  accept chunks only through a `Stream`, so a caller cannot hand them the same
  64-byte pieces. Emulating the scenario with a `MemoryStream` over one 4 096-byte
  buffer would measure the same one-shot work under a different name, so the
  scenario is `Unsupported` there rather than faked.
- **`Crc32.NET` without xxHash.** The package implements CRC-32 and CRC-32C only.
- **The three xxHash-only libraries without `Crc32`.** `K4os.Hash.xxHash`,
  `Standart.Hash.xxHash` and `HashDepot` implement no checksum.
- **`System.IO.Hashing` without the other algorithms.** It ships CRC-32, CRC-64,
  xxHash32, xxHash64, xxHash3 and xxHash128 and nothing else.
- **The three reference libraries without xxHash and CRC-32.** Each implements
  one algorithm only.

`NotApplicable` is used for no scenario in this category: every scenario is a
meaningful operation for every library, and a missing implementation is a
missing capability, not an inapplicable one.

### Cross-implementation agreement

The comparability of each scenario is recorded with its evidence, so a reader
who doubts that two libraries hash the same thing does not have to take it on
trust. On the 4 096-byte corpus with seed `0`:

| Scenario | Implementations that returned the identical value |
| --- | --- |
| `XxHash64` | System.IO.Hashing, K4os.Hash.xxHash, Standart.Hash.xxHash, HashDepot — `1A0B389290FC289C` |
| `XxHash32` | System.IO.Hashing, K4os.Hash.xxHash, Standart.Hash.xxHash, HashDepot — `F6B12790` |
| `XxHash3` | System.IO.Hashing, Standart.Hash.xxHash — `6F8AD6DCA1955037` |
| `XxHash64 Large` | System.IO.Hashing, K4os.Hash.xxHash, Standart.Hash.xxHash, HashDepot — `9D79E17926422723`, on the 1 MiB corpus |
| `Crc32` | System.IO.Hashing, Crc32.NET — `D66510DA` |

### The streaming invariant

`XxHash64 Streaming` feeds the same 4 096 bytes as 64 pieces of 64 bytes. The
xxHash algorithms are defined so that incremental hashing equals one-shot
hashing over the same bytes, and both implementations that accept caller chunks
agree on this — `System.IO.Hashing` and `K4os.Hash.xxHash` each returned
`1A0B389290FC289C`, the value of scenario 1. The scenario therefore asserts
equality with scenario 1's constant rather than with a second one: an
implementation whose incremental path disagrees with its own one-shot path is
broken, and the scenario must say so.

The measured work still includes every `Append`/`Update` call. The scenario
measures the cost of the incremental API, which is what a caller streaming a
large file pays; it does not measure a one-shot call dressed up as streaming.

### Digest scenarios return a prefix, on purpose

BLAKE2b and BLAKE3 produce 32-byte digests, and returning a `byte[]` from a
benchmarked method would allocate 32 bytes on every invocation — an allocation
caused by the harness, not by the library, that would swamp the comparison. The
scenario returns the first 8 digest bytes read as a big-endian `ulong`, which is
allocation-free and still compares the entire digest output: a library that
computes the digest incorrectly changes its prefix with overwhelming
probability, and the recorded full digests above let validation check all 32
bytes whenever that is needed.

## Measurement rules

- The corpus, the seed and every expected value are computed once, outside the
  measured method.
- A hasher instance that a library requires is constructed in the measured
  method only when the contract of the scenario is "create and hash". For
  scenarios 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 the measured method is exactly one call to
  the library's one-shot function, or one construction-plus-finalize pair where
  the library has no one-shot entry point.
- Scenario 4 constructs the hasher inside the measured method, because a
  streaming hash has to start somewhere and constructing it is part of what a
  caller streaming data pays.
- `PayloadSizeBytes` is reported for every scenario, but it belongs to the
  report and never to a chart or a point: every scenario hashes a fixed corpus,
  so the payload is identical for every library and says nothing about speed.
- No validation, formatting, hex conversion or logging happens in a measured
  method: the conditional check compiles out of benchmark builds.
- Benchmark methods return the raw integer, never a boxed value, a formatted
  string or an `object`.

## Honest limits of this category

- **Not every pair is compared.** Six rated scenarios over eight libraries
  produce an unequal number of entrants per scenario (2 to 4). `rating.md`
  documents that this is fixed by disclosure, not by exclusion, and the Scenarios
  column of the standing shows who entered.
- **The three reference libraries have no competition at all.** Their numbers
  are useful to a reader choosing between xxHash and BLAKE3 on throughput, and
  are deliberately not turned into points.
- **SIMD width dominates.** `System.IO.Hashing`, `K4os.Hash.xxHash` and
  `Standart.Hash.xxHash` all reach vectorised paths on modern hardware, so the
  differences on the small corpus are small in absolute terms and the 1 MiB
  scenario exists to separate them where throughput, not per-call overhead,
  dominates. The recorded environment of the benchmark run is the only context in
  which these numbers mean anything.
